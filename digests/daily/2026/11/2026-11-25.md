## Vector quantization and low-bit formats

### [Provably Near-Optimal PQ in the Extended Zador Model](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31579)
_Edo Liberty, Ashwin Padaki, Amir Ingber | 2026-11-23 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐⭐⭐_

For Gaussian data the paper proves SVD is the optimal preconditioning rotation for PQ. It introduces an Extended Zador estimator of k-means cost that stays accurate at low bit rates and low dimensions, and it chooses subspace segmentation by 1-d bin packing of global bit allocations. The resulting EZPQ matches OPQ, certifies a distortion lower bound per instance, and beats random-rotation methods (EDEN, E-RaBitQ, TurboQuant) on reconstruction error.

**Vector search angle:** You can replace OPQ's iterative rotation learning and naive equal-width subspaces with an SVD rotation plus variance-aware bit allocation per subspace, and you get a certificate of how far your codebooks are from optimal.

## Index structures, filtered/disk search, and partitioning theory

### [KuroANN: Avoiding Unnecessary SSD Reads in High-Recall Filtered Vector Search](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32908)
_Soobin Cho, Nakyung Lee, Jiwoong Park et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.DB) | ⭐⭐⭐_

KuroANN is an on-SSD filtered search system for partition (IVF-style) indexes. Its starting observation is that a query's filter match count is known before search and predicts how many SSD reads it needs. An offline-profiled table maps match count to the number of partitions to probe and candidates to read for a target recall, and search stops early once further reads are unlikely to help. It reports up to 34.5x over PipeANN-Filter and 9.1x over Faiss.

**Vector search angle:** Any IVF or disk index with attribute filters can adopt per-query nprobe and read budgets keyed on cheap selectivity estimates instead of fixed selectivity buckets, cutting wasted I/O at high recall.

### [Balanced Voronoi Partitions, Self-Approaching Sequences, and Applications to Efficiently Navigable Graphs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32667)
_Jonathan Conroy, Mohammadreza Daneshvaramoli, Cameron Musco et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.DS) | ⭐⭐⭐_

The paper shows that any Euclidean point set contains two points whose induced Voronoi split has balance Ω(1/d), and that this is tight. The proof bounds self-approaching sequences to length O(d). In ℓp norms with p≠2, balance can be exponentially small. As a consequence the authors construct the first navigable graphs, under a greedy-routing variant, with O(d log n) routing time, where all previous constructions needed exp(d).

**Vector search angle:** This gives theoretical grounding for two-pivot balanced splits as a partitioning primitive for hierarchical or graph indexes, and it explains why Euclidean metrics are friendlier to navigable-graph routing than other ℓp norms.

### [The Anatomy of Piecewise-Linear Learned Indexes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32049)
_Yue Chen, Yong Zhang | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.DS) | ⭐⭐_

The paper derives exact constants for keys covered per segment in PLA learned indexes such as PGM, FITing-Tree and RadixSpline. It disproves the O(log log n) height heuristic for PGM under endpoint promotion. Its proposed rank-equispaced pivot promotion achieves log log height with Θ(n/ε²) space, and the core theorems are formalized in Lean.

**Vector search angle:** RE-PGM is a sharper learned index for the 1-D sorted structures inside a vector database, such as payload and attribute indexes used for pre-filtering, ID-to-offset maps on disk, and sorted posting lists in sparse or hybrid search.

### [On Euclidean Diameter in Dynamic Streams](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33139)
_Jeff Giliberti, MohammadTaghi Hajiaghayi | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.DS) | ⭐_

This is a one-pass streaming algorithm that maintains a c-approximate (c>√3) diameter witness for high-dimensional points under insertions and deletions. It uses sublinear space and poly(d, log N) update time, built on Gaussian hashing through shrinking caps and local and global rebuild schedules. A communication lower bound shows that any constant-factor approximation needs polynomial memory.

**Vector search angle:** The cap-based Gaussian hashing hierarchy with single-leaf updates is a furthest-neighbor and LSH-style design pattern for dynamic insert/delete workloads, but the paper offers no direct ANN result.

### [PHM-ensemble: Scalable and Stable Probabilistic Multiresolution Clustering via Ensemble Density Estimation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32945)
_Ali Turfah, Xiaoquan Wen | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

PHM-ensemble pools mixture-model fits from an ensemble to estimate density, then merges components hierarchically into a multiresolution clustering. It is more stable across runs than common baselines. On 1.82M cells it beats Leiden in agreement with expert labels at half the compute time.

**Vector search angle:** Ensemble-pooled mixtures merged into a hierarchy could inspire more stable, multi-level coarse partitions for hierarchical IVF, though the paper does not evaluate on embedding-scale data or measure retrieval.

## Embeddings and retrieval models that change what is stored

### [OmniEVIE: Evidence-Vector-Informed Embedding for Multi-modal Retrieval](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32203)
_Zifei Wang, Wei Wen, Qiang Ji et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐⭐⭐_

OmniEVIE is a 4.5B multi-vector embedding model for text, images, visual documents, video and tools. Each record is capped at 128 int8 vectors of dimension 64 (9.5 KB), with candidates compressed during training (ST-HAC) to match the index format. Retrieval runs in three stages: a mean vector, then 1-bit signs, then int8 MaxSim. At 5M records it is 25x faster than exact 4096-d single-vector search and 532x faster than full int8 MaxSim, while staying near full-precision quality.

**Vector search angle:** It is a concrete reference design for compressed late-interaction serving: capped token count, low dimension, int8 storage, and a coarse-to-fine cascade of mean vector, binary sketch and int8 MaxSim, trained to match the index format.

### [RELAY: Learning Compatible Representations One Encoder at a Time](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31882)
_Yu Wang, Chuan Qin, Qiang Zhang | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐⭐_

RELAY handles embedding-model upgrades in two steps. It first trains a new query encoder with a learned projection against the old gallery, then freezes both and trains the new document encoder, so one query vector can score old and new document embeddings during incremental re-encoding. On text-to-image upgrades it improves Recall@1 on un-re-encoded galleries by 8.7-10.2 points and ends within about 1 point of joint training.

**Vector search angle:** This supports zero-downtime model upgrades for a live index, where mixed-version vectors coexist in one collection during backfill; it also shows that score calibration per document version matters.

### [Small Learned Projections for Semantic-Reversal-Aware Retrieval with Spectral Acceleration: A Four-Domain Empirical Study](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31442)
_Sayed Amir Karim | 2026-11-23 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐_

A tiny learned projection from 1024 dimensions down to 11-32 is trained on declared near-duplicate cases where a small edit reverses meaning. On those declared cases it keeps the meaning-reversing near-duplicates out of the top-10 across four domains and pools up to 100k, under exact search, while retaining correct paraphrases 93-99% of the time. The claims are scoped to declared cases, transfer across domains is partial, and the spectral accelerator is closed-source.

**Vector search angle:** The paper argues that many small task-specific projected indexes per domain or tenant (about 90x smaller) can replace one large store, but its evidence is narrow and uses only exact search.

### [Speculative Decoding for Semantic-ID-Based Generative Recommendations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32855)
_Harshal A. Chaudhari | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐⭐_

SIDECAR replaces constrained beam search over Semantic-ID tries with draft candidates scored in a single tree-attention pass. A branch-and-bound fallback can certify the exact top-K within at most L model calls. Under a fixed budget it reports an approximation factor, which allows quality-aware early stopping.

**Vector search angle:** Generative retrieval over quantized Semantic IDs is a competitor to ANN indexes, and its certified-top-K and announced-error-factor idea mirrors bound-based early termination you could expose in tree, IVF or quantized index search.

## LLM-side quantization, KV retrieval, and memory tiers

### [Shifter Quantization: Compact Representations for Native Integer Computation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33080)
_Yufeng Huang | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐⭐_

Shifter Quantization rebuilds native-width integer operands from groupwise-quantized values by appending and shifting shared bits. This lets standard integer MMA, including INT4 kernels via the SHI variant, span full dot products despite per-group scales. It improves both error and throughput over llama.cpp, NInfer and vLLM FP8 baselines at 2, 4 and 8 bits.

**Vector search angle:** Groupwise scalar quantization of vectors normally forces per-group float rescaling in distance kernels; shared-bit shifting could let finer-grained SQ codes still use a single native int8/int4 dot product.

### [MXFP4 for LLM Inference: Complementary Post-Training Quantization Techniques and Their Interactions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32873)
_Natalia Frumkin, Alireza Khodamoradi, Kristof Denolf et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐⭐_

This is a systematic study of how Hadamard rotation granularity, PTQ algorithm and scale selection interact with MXFP6, MXFP4 and NVFP4 across six LLMs. Rotation helps MXFP4, which has coarse power-of-two scales, much more than NVFP4, which has finer scales. The authors also propose Midmax, a calibration-free scale rule that reduces clipping error.

**Vector search angle:** The finding that rotation matters less as block scales get finer carries over to vector SQ design. Hardware-native FP4 microscaling formats are also a candidate low-bit storage format for embeddings, with GPU tensor-core distance computation.

### [HiCal: Offline Calibration for Hierarchical Scale Encoding in Accurate HiF4 Quantization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32624)
_Zhiyuan Zhang, Yanzhao Li, Hui Dong et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

HiCal calibrates offline the rounding bias and scale-extension thresholds of HiFloat4's three-level hierarchical block scales. This cuts quantization MSE by 10-23% with no per-block candidate search at inference. It gains about 1 point on Qwen3 benchmarks.

**Vector search angle:** Fitting the scale thresholds of a hierarchical block quantizer offline to the data distribution is a cheap trick that applies equally to block-scaled embedding codes.

### [AVQ-Attention-2: Adaptive Vector-Quantized Attention with Exact Local Blocks and Linear-Cost Training](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32989)
_Winfried van den Dool, Patrick Forré, Amir Habibian et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐⭐_

AVQ-Attention-2 clusters keys and attends to cluster means rather than codewords, adapting codebook resolution to where each query's attention mass falls. It combines this with exact local attention. The method trains in O(NM), drops into pretrained models without fine-tuning, and runs faster than FlashAttention at near-dense quality.

**Vector search angle:** Query-adaptive cluster refinement (coarse centroids far away, fine resolution near the query) is essentially adaptive IVF over the KV cache; using cluster means as aggregate stand-ins suggests similar summary-based pruning for partition scoring.

### [When Exact Token Importance Is Not Enough: Limits of Top-K Selection in LLM Attention](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32324)
_Irmaan Mirzanejad | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

Ranking KV positions by individual importance and keeping the top K can be arbitrarily worse than the best joint set, because attention renormalizes over whatever set is kept. This holds even when each position's importance is measured exactly. Audits on SmolLM2, Gemma and Qwen confirm frequent gaps, and a one-swap audit (SwapCert) finds improving sets.

**Vector search angle:** If you build ANN-backed retrieval attention, perfect top-K recall of keys does not guarantee output fidelity, so success should be judged by set quality, not per-key recall.

### [Mayfly: Lifecycle-Aware High-Bandwidth Flash for Recursive Self-Improvement](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32264)
_Chunlin Tian, Mingjia Fan, Feng Yu et al. | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.AR) | ⭐_

Mayfly manages KV-cache overflow onto High-Bandwidth Flash for bursty agent workloads, where sessions grow their caches together and then release them. It caps per-step flash reads while HBM is full and moves data back to HBM as sessions end. It also keeps one valid flash copy per block and trims it when the orchestrator announces a session end, avoiding write amplification. It finishes 23-55% sooner than simpler policies.

**Vector search angle:** High-Bandwidth Flash is an emerging tier between HBM and SSD that GPU vector indexes could use. The paper's per-step read-budget and keep-one-flash-copy policies carry over to tiered placement of index segments.
