## ANN systems & incremental indexes

### [Rethinking ANNS on Commodity PIM: PIM-Native Vector Search with Cooperative Filtering](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27010)
_Dawei Guo, Yizou Chen, Fan Yang et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.AR) | ⭐⭐⭐_

PIVOT redesigns ANNS for UPMEM processing-in-memory hardware. Distance evaluation uses binary codes and bitwise ops, with no lookup tables. A survivor-mass model derives a conservative threshold that is used for both CPU-side cluster filtering and PIM-side lower-bound filtering, removing per-core top-k state; the CPU handles coordination and reranking. It reports up to 18.8x the QPS of CPU and 10.2x prior PIM systems, with higher recall.

**Vector search angle:** Two ideas transfer beyond PIM: replacing per-worker top-k heaps with a globally derived distance threshold, and splitting work into a binary-code filter followed by full-precision CPU rerank. Both apply to SIMD, GPU or near-storage IVF scanners where heap maintenance and output bandwidth dominate.

### [LongCat Recall Sparse Attention: Co-Designing Key Organization and Retrieval for Long-Context Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27233)
_Hao Zhang, Bin Xiao, Kefeng Zhang | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.CL) | ⭐⭐_

LRSA speeds up top-K key selection for sparse attention by building an incremental index over the KV history, integrated into SGLang. History is split into time buckets that are indexed independently as keys arrive. Within each bucket, keys are grouped into capacity-balanced directional groups, a fixed number of complete groups is packed per page, and each group keeps its own center for page selection before members are refined. It reports 4.70x faster key selection than DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) and 35.6x faster selection plus attention than full attention at 1M tokens.

**Vector search angle:** Its design is a GPU-resident streaming IVF that never repartitions old data: append-only time buckets, balanced partitions, page-aligned member layout, and fine-grained centroids as page routers. These patterns are directly relevant to freshness, segment design and coarse-to-fine routing in vector DBs.

### [MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973)
_Chang Nie, Chaoyou Fu, Songlin Liu et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.CL) | ⭐_

MemOnce caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory and decodes them back into KV caches on demand. Memory sparse attention retrieves relevant memory entries with indices computed once and shared across layers. Adapting Qwen3.5-4B, it reports a 1313x smaller memory-state cache and 6.9x lower prefill latency at 1M context.

**Vector search angle:** The connection is loose: it is another case of retrieval over a compact learned memory store, where computing sparse indices once and sharing them across layers resembles single-pass retrieval with multiple downstream consumers.

## Clustering for partition building

### [Highly Efficient Vertical Federated Clustering with Triangle-Inequality-based Pruning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27117)
_Feiteng Nie, Shengkun Zhu, Zepeng Liu et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.DB) | ⭐⭐_

TriCD is an exact pruning method for coordinate-descent k-means (CDKM). It builds triangle-inequality bounds on demand to skip redundant objective evaluations, uses negligible extra space, and produces the same assignments as unpruned CDKM. It reaches up to 50x speedup over Lloyd's algorithm; FedTriCD extends it to vertically partitioned federated data with fewer communication rounds.

**Vector search angle:** The non-federated TriCD core is a fast, exact, low-memory k-means variant that could cut IVF centroid training and PQ codebook build time on large embedding sets.

### [Federated and Balanced $k$-means: Communication Efficiency, Privacy, and Heterogeneity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27114)
_Feiteng Nie, Shengkun Zhu, Sheng Wang et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.DB) | ⭐_

Fabric is a balanced, privacy-preserving federated k-means. It splits the problem into per-client subproblems and enforces balance constraints with the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), adding calibrated noise for privacy. It reports 29% lower SSE on average, 1.41x better balance and 26x faster runtime than prior methods.

**Vector search angle:** The ADMM-based balanced-cluster formulation could be lifted out of the federated setting to build size-balanced IVF partitions or shards, which reduces tail latency from oversized posting lists.

## Quantization of generic vectors (KV cache, activations, VQ)

### [CarrierKV: An Edge LLM Accelerator with Positional Carrier Decomposition for Low-Bit KV Cache](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26857)
_Junyi Luo, Xinting Jiang, Ruichen Qi et al. | 2026-11-19 | arXiv (cs.AR) | ⭐⭐_

CarrierKV observes that most key energy is a per-head 'positional carrier' vector that RoPE rotates with position. In NVFP4, this carrier sets the shared block scale and costs about 5 dB (nearly one bit) of signal-to-quantization-noise ratio. The method removes the carrier before RoPE, quantizes only the content residual to 4 bits, and computes attention scores directly on the codes while hardware regenerates the carrier's score term. It cuts 3-bit TurboQuant key degradation dramatically (perplexity increase 69.7 to 0.76).

**Vector search angle:** The lesson is general: subtract a dominant shared component before block-scaled or rotated quantization and add its inner-product term back analytically. This matches mean-centering and residual tricks for scalar, RaBitQ and PQ codes on anisotropic embeddings, and shows that Hadamard rotation can hurt when one direction dominates.

### [PLoQ: A Prompt-Specific Low-Rank Basis for 4-bit Quantization of LLM Activations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27092)
_Faraz Tahmasebi, Michael Pellauer, Hyoukjun Kwon et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

PLoQ estimates a low-rank basis of dominant activation directions from each prompt during prefill and reuses it while decoding. Those components are quantized separately and restored inside the GEMM, with no weight changes. At about 4.2 bits per activation it beats state-of-the-art mixed-precision quantization on Llama and Qwen models from 1B to 70B.

**Vector search angle:** The analogue is data-adaptive outlier subspace extraction: keep a few dominant PCA directions at higher precision and quantize the residual at low bits. This is a viable scheme for per-segment or per-tenant adaptive scalar quantization of embeddings.

### [Exploring the Potential of Ultra-Low-Bit Inference on LLM Accelerators](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27561)
_Hao Wang, Yang Shi, Jiageng Shi et al. | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.AR) | ⭐_

VQACC repurposes the existing operand buffers of systolic-array LLM accelerators to perform vector-quantization codebook lookups, folding dequantization into the normal operand-addressing dataflow. It reaches 2.63x speedup over the EVA baseline with 0.17% added area.

**Vector search angle:** This is relevant to anyone mapping PQ/VQ lookup-table distance computation onto matrix accelerators: it shows codebook lookups can ride existing buffer ports rather than needing dedicated LUT hardware.

## Embedding model migration

### [ERA: Embed, Route, Adapt for Cold-Start Cross-Model Latent Retrieval](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26919)
_Venkata Chivukula, Zifeng Wang, Jinsung Yoon | 2026-11-20 | arXiv (cs.IR) | ⭐⭐_

ERA lets queries from a new encoder search an index built with an old encoder without re-embedding the corpus. It picks a small set of anchor documents via cosine farthest-point sampling and trains an orthogonally regularized adapter to map between embedding spaces. A router sends the rare hard queries to the original encoder. It reports up to 35% relative nDCG@10 gains on Arguana and end-to-end latency cut from roughly 160 ms to 38-86 ms.

**Vector search angle:** This bears on index lifecycle: query-side adapters with farthest-point anchor selection offer a cheap path for encoder upgrades or cross-model querying without rebuilding HNSW/IVF indexes. Orthogonal regularization helps keep the index's distance geometry intact.
