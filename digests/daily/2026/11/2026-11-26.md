## Quantization and code assignment

### [Any-Rate: Half-Bit Multi-Precision LLM Quantization with Coded Bit-Planes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34402)
_Hoonki Lee, Haechan Shin, Hyuntaek Jung et al. | 2026-11-25 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐⭐_

Any-Rate is a nested multi-precision weight quantizer that serves 2-8 bits from a single checkpoint, in half-bit steps over 2-4 bits. It constrains some bit-planes to extended Hamming codewords over 8-weight blocks (0.5 bits/weight) and shares one LUT per row across precisions. It jointly optimizes every precision against its own target and decodes about as fast as existing kernels.

**Vector search angle:** The coded bit-plane layout transfers to vector codes: store one bit-plane-ordered code per vector, scan leading planes (even half-bit ones) for coarse distances, then read further planes for refinement. This gives fractional-bit tiers between 1-bit/RaBitQ-style codes and 4-bit scalar codes without storing multiple copies.

### [Same Tokenizer, Better Tokens: Choosing Image Tokens by MAP Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33261)
_Piotr Kubaty, Jacek Tabor, Łukasz Struski | 2026-11-24 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

The paper replaces independent nearest-code assignment in VQ image tokenizers with a joint search. It solves a closest-vector problem under a decoder-dependent metric derived from a local model of reconstruction loss. RecaP also adds an autoregressive prior to favor predictable code sequences, improving PSNR and early-checkpoint FID.

**Vector search angle:** This reinforces the ScaNN-style lesson that assigning codes by nearest Euclidean centroid is suboptimal when the downstream objective differs. Encoding PQ/additive codes against a loss-aware metric (e.g., an inner-product-error metric) rather than plain L2 can lower retrieval error at a fixed code size.

### [BReD: Block Replay Dithering for Stable Low-Bit EMA Optimizer States](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33951)
_Heshen Zhan, Youhan Huang, Yunke Peng et al. | 2026-11-25 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐_

Repeated dequantize-update-requantize cycles on EMA optimizer states accumulate biased rounding error and amplify variance. BReD counters this with subtractive dithering, using deterministic seed replay and block-wise shared dither values, so no random tensors need to be stored. With it, 4-bit states match full precision and 3-bit states still converge stably.

**Vector search angle:** Seed-replayed block dither is a storage-free way to debias scalar quantization. It is relevant wherever vectors or centroids are repeatedly requantized, such as streaming updates to quantized centroids, online codebook refinement, or running-mean statistics in mutable indexes.

## Dimensionality reduction and clustering theory

### [RAP: Radial-Angular Random Projection for Hyperbolic Representations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34298)
_Jiahong Liu, Wenhao Yu, Yifan Li et al. | 2026-11-25 | arXiv (cs.LG) | ⭐⭐_

RAP is an oblivious hyperbolic-to-hyperbolic random projection that keeps the radial coordinate exactly. It applies a Gaussian JL projection only to the angular direction, which reduces the problem to angular JL on the sphere, with explicit sinh-conditioned distortion bounds. It cuts distortion by about 48% over hyperbolic baselines, scales to millions of points, and improves vision-language retrieval.

**Vector search angle:** The radial/angular split (store the norm exactly, compress the direction) is a concrete recipe for compressing hyperbolic embeddings before indexing. It mirrors norm-plus-direction tricks used in MIPS and RaBitQ, so you can apply angular quantization or sketches to the directional part only.

### [Near-Tight Robust Coresets for Clustering in Euclidean Spaces](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34063)
_Pengsen Huang, Yi Li, Xuan Wu | 2026-11-25 | arXiv (cs.DS) | ⭐⭐_

The authors prove near-tight worst-case size bounds, over all ambient dimensions, for coresets of robust (k,z)-clustering with m outliers. Their coresets approximate the cost for every center set and every outlier budget up to m. The size is Θ(m) plus a term depending on k, ε and m that is nearly optimal for each regime of z.

**Vector search angle:** Robust coresets let you train IVF partitions or PQ codebooks with k-means on a small weighted sample while tolerating outliers. The bounds tell you how large the sample must be for a given ε and outlier budget m when building indexes over noisy, heavy-tailed embedding sets.

## Multi-stage retrieval and reranking

### [When Rerankers Work Together: Better and Faster Reranking without Joint Training](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33844)
_Yanqiao Chen, Dongsheng Hou, Yuhan Rui | 2026-11-25 | arXiv (cs.CL) | ⭐_

The paper studies cascades of heterogeneous, separately trained rerankers spanning lexical, single-vector, late-interaction and cross-encoder scoring. It introduces two cacheable, linear-time coarse scorers, Cache-Pointwise and Cache-Shapley. Placing these ahead of cross-encoders, or fusing them with ColBERTv2 via reciprocal rank fusion, improves accuracy by 4-9 points with 1.4-1.9x speedups, though the gains depend heavily on stage order and candidate budget.

**Vector search angle:** Its stage-removal methodology (measuring evidence retention, repair and breakage per stage) is a useful template for tuning candidate budgets between ANN retrieval, quantized rerank and late-interaction rescoring. Cacheable passage representations argue for storing precomputed scorer features alongside vectors.
